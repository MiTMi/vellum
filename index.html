Everything a workspace should be

A block editor

Type / for headings, lists, code, math, callouts and embeds. Every block drags, nests and copies as a deep link.

Databases & views

Table, board, calendar, gallery and timeline over the same rows. Relations, rollups and formulas compute as you look at them.

Offline-first

Reads and writes hit a local replica first, so the app never waits on the network. Queued edits replay when you reconnect.

Publish to web

One toggle turns a page into a public link. Unpublishing revokes it permanently — the link is the access control.

⌘K everything

Full-text search with contextual snippets, plus the actions you reach for most — new page, new database, theme, trash.

Native Mac app

The same workspace as a real desktop app: native menus, Touch ID unlock, proper PDF export and its own Dock icon.

Write

Write with blocks, not boxes.

Everything on a page is a block, and every block is one keystroke away. Press / for headings, checklists, code, LaTeX, callouts, tables of contents or a live embed.

Mention another page with @ and it stays in sync — rename the target and every mention follows.

Organize

Databases that think with you.

A database is just a page whose children are rows — so every row opens as a full document, with its own content and comments.

Point a relation at another database, roll its values up, or write a formula like prop("Points") * 2. Computed columns are evaluated as you look at them, never stored stale.

Sync

Works offline. Syncs everywhere.

Vellum keeps a full replica of your workspace on every device. Reads come from the replica, writes land there instantly and queue in a durable outbox.

Reconnect and the queue replays in order — so the Mac app and the browser converge on the same workspace without you thinking about it once.

Share

Share when you're ready.

Flip one switch and a page gets an unguessable public link that anyone can read — no account, no app, just the page.

Links to your other pages render as plain titles, so a published page can never leak the private ones. Unpublish and the URL is dead for good.
