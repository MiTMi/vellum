Getting started

Vellum is a personal workspace for notes, documents and databases. One person owns it, everything lives on your device first, and nothing is shared until you decide to share it.

¶Signing in

A Vellum workspace has exactly one account — the owner’s. The first time you open the app you’ll see “Claim your workspace”: enter your email, choose a password, and the account is yours. After that the same screen says “Welcome back” and simply asks you to sign in.

Your password needs all five of these:

  • At least 12 characters
  • A lowercase letter
  • An uppercase letter
  • A number
  • A symbol, e.g. ! ? #

The checklist on the sign-up screen ticks each rule off as you type, so you always know what’s missing. On a Mac with Touch ID you can tick Enable Touch ID sign-in on this Mac while signing in, and next time a fingerprint is enough.

¶The four parts of the window

  • The sidebar (left) — Search, your recent and favourite pages, templates, the Vault, and the tree of all your pages under Private. Its footer has New page, New database, Trash and Settings.
  • The tab bar (top) — one tab per page you have open, exactly like a browser.
  • The top bar — back and forward arrows, breadcrumbs showing where you are, when the page was last edited, and the Share, favourite, theme and “…” buttons.
  • The page — a title and, under it, an editor made of blocks.

¶Your first page

  1. Press ⌘N, or click New page at the bottom of the sidebar.
  2. Type a title. It appears in the sidebar as you type — there is no Save button anywhere in Vellum.
  3. Press Enter to drop from the title into the body, and start writing.
  4. Press / whenever you want something that isn’t a paragraph: a heading, a list, a table, an image, an equation, a video.
¶

Everything saves itself. Edits are written to your device immediately and sync in the background. Close the window mid-sentence and it will be there when you come back.

¶Where to go next

  • Writing on a page — the editor, top to bottom.
  • Databases: the basics — when a list of pages wants to be a table.
  • Keyboard shortcuts — the whole list on one screen.

Writing on a page

A Vellum page is a stack of blocks. A paragraph is a block, so is a heading, a to-do, an image, a table. Every block can be moved, nested and linked to on its own.

¶The title

The big line at the top is the page title. It’s what shows in the sidebar, in search results and in breadcrumbs. Press Enter in the title and the cursor jumps into the body.

¶Type / for anything

Typing / at the start of an empty line opens the block menu. Keep typing to filter it — /quote, /todo, /embed — then press Enter to insert what’s highlighted. The whole catalogue is in the block library.

¶Markdown shortcuts

These work as you type, no menu needed:

Type thisand you get
# Heading 1
## Heading 2
### Heading 3
- A bullet list
1. A numbered list
[] A to-do with a checkbox
> A quote
**bold**bold text
*italic*italic text

Press Enter on an empty list item to leave the list, and Tab / ⇧Tab to indent and outdent one.

¶Moving blocks around

Hover a block and two controls appear in the left margin: a + that adds a block underneath, and a ⠿ handle. Drag the handle to move the block anywhere on the page; click it for a menu that can delete the block or change its type.

Select some text and a formatting toolbar appears above it — bold, italic, underline, strikethrough, code, colour, and a link button.

¶Copy a link to one block

Hover any block and a small link button appears in the right-hand margin: Copy link to block. The link it copies points at that exact block, so opening it navigates to the page, scrolls to the block and flashes it. It works in the browser and in the Mac app.

¶Making a page look right

The … button in the top right holds the page’s appearance options:

  • Default / Serif / Mono — the typeface for this page only.
  • Small text — tightens the type for dense notes.
  • Full width — lets the page use the whole window instead of a column.
  • Lock page — makes it read-only so you can’t edit it by accident. A 🔒 Page locked note appears at the top; switch it off to edit again.

The bottom of that menu always shows the page’s word count and when it was last edited.

¶Icons and covers

  1. Hover just above the title and click Add icon. Pick any emoji — it shows in the sidebar, in tabs and in search.
  2. Click Add cover for a banner image. Ten gradients come built in, or upload your own picture.
  3. Click the icon or Change cover at any time to swap or remove either.
¶

Uploaded images are shrunk for you. Anything you paste or upload is resized to at most 1600px on its long edge and re-encoded as WebP, which usually makes it five to forty times smaller. GIFs, SVGs and already-small files pass through untouched.

Linking pages together

Vellum has three ways to point at another page, and all three keep themselves up to date: rename the target and every link follows.

¶Mention a page inline: @

  1. While writing a sentence, type @.
  2. Keep typing part of the page’s name to narrow the list.
  3. Press Enter. A little chip with the page’s icon and title lands mid-sentence.

Click the chip to open that page. If you later rename it, the chip shows the new name automatically.

¶Link on its own line

/link to page inserts the same link as a block of its own — useful for a list of related documents. /sub-page goes one step further: it creates a new page inside the current one, links to it and opens it.

¶Linked mentions

At the bottom of every page is a Linked mentions section listing each page that links here. You never maintain it; it appears as soon as something points at this page and disappears when nothing does. Click the header to fold it away.

¶Links to a single block

To point at one paragraph rather than a whole page, hover the block and click Copy link to block in the right margin. Paste that link anywhere — opening it scrolls straight to the block and flashes it.

¶

The Vault stays out of it. Encrypted pages never appear in the @ list, because a mention in an ordinary page would give away that the secret page exists. Link to Vault pages from inside the Vault instead.

Pages & the sidebar

Every page can hold other pages, so your workspace is a tree. The sidebar is where you shape it.

¶What’s in the sidebar

  • Search — the same thing as ⌘K.
  • Library — every page in one table. See below.
  • Recents — the five pages you touched last.
  • Favorites — appears once you star something.
  • Templates — appears once you mark a page as a template.
  • Vault — your encrypted corner. See The Vault.
  • Private — the full tree of your pages.

¶The Library

Library in the sidebar opens a table of your whole workspace: page name, where each page lives (its parent, or Private for top-level pages), when it was last edited, and when you last visited it. Four tabs slice it — Recents, Favorites, Private and Templates — and the magnifying glass filters by title. Click a row to open the page, or its source to jump to the parent. Visit times are per device; Vault pages never appear here.

¶Making pages

  • ⌘N or New page in the footer — a page at the top level.
  • Hover any page in the tree and click + — a page inside it.
  • Right-click New page (or click the database icon beside it) to choose page or database.
  • /sub-page in the editor — a page inside the one you’re writing.

¶Rearranging the tree

  1. Drag a page onto another to file it inside — the target opens so you can see it land.
  2. Drag between two pages to reorder them; a line shows where it will go.
  3. Drag onto empty space under the tree to bring a page back to the top level.
  4. Click the › chevron beside a page to fold its children away.

If dragging is awkward, use … › Move to in the top bar: search for the destination page, or pick Move to top level. Pages inside the one you’re moving are never offered — a page can’t contain itself.

¶The page’s own menu

Hover a page in the sidebar and click … for:

  • Add to / Remove from favorites — the star in the top bar does the same.
  • Rename — edit the name in place; Esc cancels.
  • Duplicate — a copy, with everything inside it. ⌘D does this for the page you’re on.
  • Move to trash.

¶The trash

Deleting is never immediate. Move to Trash takes the page (and everything inside it) out of the tree; the bin icon in the sidebar footer shows what’s in there, with the date each item was deleted.

  • Restore puts a page back where it was and opens it.
  • Delete forever asks a second time — click it twice.
  • Empty trash clears everything, also with a confirm click.

¶Resizing and hiding the sidebar

Drag its right edge to make it wider or narrower; the width is remembered. ⌘\ hides it entirely and brings it back.

Search & the ⌘K palette

⌘K is the fastest way to reach anything: it searches your pages, runs commands, and creates pages that don’t exist yet.

¶Finding a page

  1. Press ⌘K (or click Search in the sidebar).
  2. Start typing. Vellum searches titles and the text inside pages.
  3. Each result shows the trail of pages it lives under, plus a snippet with your words highlighted.
  4. Move with ↑ ↓ and press Enter; Esc closes.

Open it with the box empty and you get your six most recently edited pages instead — usually all you need.

¶Running commands

The Actions section at the top always offers:

  • New page
  • New database
  • Switch to dark / light mode
  • Open trash
  • Settings
  • New from template: … — one entry per template you’ve made.

Actions match on more than their name: typing “theme”, “kanban” or “bin” finds the right one.

¶Creating from the search box

The last row is always Create page “…” with whatever you typed. Press Enter on it and you get a new page already titled — the quickest way to capture a thought.

¶

The Vault is excluded. Encrypted pages are never indexed, so nothing inside them can surface here by its text. While the Vault is unlocked you can still find those pages by title — matched in memory on your device, never on the server.

Tabs & getting around

Vellum keeps several pages open at once, remembers where you were, and gives every page a trail back to where it lives.

¶Tabs

  • ⌘T or the + at the end of the strip opens a tab.
  • Click a tab to switch; click its × (or middle-click the tab) to close it.
  • In the Mac app, ⌘W closes the current tab — and the window, once it’s the last one.
  • Each tab keeps its own back/forward history.
  • Your tabs come back after a reload or a restart, still on the same pages.

¶Back, forward, breadcrumbs

The arrows at the top left step through the pages you’ve visited in this tab. Beside them, the breadcrumbs show where the current page sits — click any step to go there. A deep trail is shortened with an ellipsis so the bar never overflows.

¶Row peek

Opening a database row doesn’t navigate anywhere: it floats the row over the table so you keep your place. Esc or a click outside closes it. See Databases.

¶Light and dark

The sun/moon button in the top bar flips the theme, and so does Settings › Appearance and the ⌘K action. Vellum starts on whichever your system prefers and then remembers your choice.

Databases: the basics

A database is a page whose children are rows — so every row is itself a full page, with its own content, sub-pages and comments. Tasks, reading lists, contacts, a CRM: same building block.

¶Making one

  1. Click the database icon in the sidebar footer (or right-click New page) and choose New database. ⌘K › New database works too.
  2. Give it a title. It opens in Table view.
  3. It starts with three columns: Status (Not started / In progress / Done), Tags and Date. Change or delete them freely.

To put a database inside a document instead, type /database in the editor.

¶Adding rows

  • In Table view, click + New under the last row and type the name straight into the cell.
  • In any view, the + New button in the toolbar adds a row and opens it. In Calendar view the new row is dated today.
  • When the table is grouped, each group header has its own + — the new row already has that group’s value.

¶Filling in cells

  • Text / number / URL — click and type; Enter commits.
  • Select and multi-select — click for the list of options. Type a name that doesn’t exist yet and Create appears; the new option joins the column for good. Click a colour dot in the property menu to recolour one.
  • Date — pick a day; tick End date to turn it into a range, which is what the Timeline view draws.
  • Checkbox — click it.

¶Opening a row

Hover a row and click Open. The row appears in a floating panel: its properties at the top, then an ordinary editor for the body. Write as much as you like in there — a row is a real page. Esc closes the panel.

¶Columns

  • Add — the + at the right end of the header row.
  • Rename or retype — click the column header. See Property types.
  • Resize — drag the edge of a header.
  • Delete — Delete property at the bottom of the column menu.

¶Deleting rows

Hover a row and click the bin at the end of the name cell. Rows go to the trash like any other page, so they can be restored.

Property types

Click any column header to rename it or change its type. There are twelve.

¶Changing a type later

You can, at any time, from the column menu. Values that no longer make sense in the new type simply stop being shown rather than being thrown away — switch back and they return.

Views, filters & sorting

The same rows, as many ways of looking at them as you like. Every database starts with one tab per layout; each tab is a saved view carrying its own filters, sorts and grouping, synced with the workspace.

¶The five layouts

ViewGood for
TableEverything at once: one row per record, one column per property.
BoardA kanban board, one column per value of a select property. Drag cards between columns to change that value.
CalendarA month grid, placing each row on its date.
GalleryCards — nice when rows have covers or images.
TimelineA Gantt chart drawn from date ranges. Rows without a date are listed underneath rather than dropped.

When a database has more than one select column, Board gets a menu for choosing which one makes the columns. Calendar and Timeline get the same menu for choosing which date column they follow — each view remembers its own choice.

¶Managing views

Click the + at the end of the tab strip to add a view, picking its layout. Click the active tab to open its menu: rename it, switch its layout, duplicate it (filters and sorts come along), or delete it. Which tab you’re on is remembered per device; the views themselves sync everywhere.

¶Filtering

  1. Click the filter icon in the toolbar.
  2. Pick a column — every property can be filtered, Name included.
  3. Choose an operator suited to the column’s type — contains for text, > for numbers, is before for dates, is any of for selects — and give it a value.

Add filter stacks more rules, joined by one And/Or choice. Add group nests a second set with its own And/Or, so “done and (urgent or overdue)” is expressible. A chip under the toolbar counts the active rules next to a count like 1 of 3; its × clears them all.

¶Sorting

The sort icon lists Name plus every property. Clicking one cycles ascending → descending → off, and you can stack several — the first rule wins, later ones break ties. Each active sort shows as its own chip. You can also sort from a column’s own menu.

¶Grouping (Table)

Click Group and choose a select, multi-select or checkbox column. Rows gather under a header per value, each with a count, and each header has a + that makes a new row already carrying that value. Click a header to fold the group away; choose No grouping to undo it.

¶Searching inside a database

The magnifying glass in the toolbar filters rows by name as you type. Esc clears it.

¶

Views sync; your vantage point doesn’t. Filters, sorts and grouping belong to the view and follow it to every device. Which tab you’re looking at, which groups you’ve folded away and the in-database search stay with the device you set them on.

Relations, rollups & formulas

Three ways for a database to work things out for itself: point at another database, summarise what you find there, and calculate from the values in a row.

¶Relations

  1. Add a column and set its type to Relation.
  2. Pick the database it should point at, under Related database.
  3. Close the menu and click a cell in that column: search for rows and tick the ones to link.

Linked rows appear as chips; click one to jump to it. Rename a linked row and the chip renames itself. Delete it and the chip quietly disappears rather than dangling.

¶Rollups

A rollup follows one of your relation columns and summarises a property of the rows on the other end.

  1. Add a column and set its type to Rollup.
  2. Choose which relation to follow.
  3. Choose which property of the related rows to look at (or Name).
  4. Choose the calculation.
CalculationResult
Count allHow many rows are linked.
Count valuesHow many of them actually have a value.
Sum / Average / Min / MaxThe usual arithmetic, for numbers.
Percent checkedShare of linked rows whose checkbox is ticked.
Show originalThe values themselves, listed.

¶Formulas

Set a column’s type to Formula and write an expression. Refer to another column with prop("Name") — the chips under the box insert one for you. Errors are shown as you type, and the column reads Error until the expression makes sense.

Examples that work today:

  • prop("Points") * 2
  • if(prop("Done"), "✅", "…")
  • concat(prop("First"), " ", prop("Last"))
  • dateDiff(prop("Due"), today())
  • round(prop("Total") / prop("Count"))

Operators

+ - * / % ^ for arithmetic; == != < <= > >= for comparisons; && || ! for logic.

Functions

GroupFunctions
Logicif, not, empty
Textconcat, join, length, lower, upper, contains, replace, slice, format
Numbersabs, round, floor, ceil, sqrt, min, max, sum, number
Datesnow, today, year, month, day, dateDiff, dateAdd
¶

Nothing computed is ever stored. Rollups and formulas are worked out each time they’re shown, so they update the moment anything they depend on changes. Two formulas that refer to each other stop instead of looping forever.

Templates

Any page can become a template. Meeting notes, a weekly review, a project skeleton — build it once, then start from it.

¶Making a template

  1. Build the page the way you want every copy to start: headings, checklists, an icon, a cover, even sub-pages.
  2. Open … in the top bar and switch Template on.
  3. The page moves into a Templates section in the sidebar. It’s still an ordinary page — edit it whenever the template needs to change.

¶Three ways to use one

  • Hover the template in the sidebar and click + — a fresh copy at the top level, opened for you.
  • On any empty page, pick from the Start with a template row that appears above the editor.
  • ⌘K › New from template: ….

A copy is a normal page: it carries the content, icon, cover and sub-pages across, and it isn’t a template itself. Editing it never touches the original.

¶

Switching Template back off returns the page to the ordinary tree, exactly where it was.

Page history & comments

Vellum keeps older versions of your writing, and lets you leave notes for yourself on any page.

¶Going back in time

  1. Open … › Page history.
  2. Versions are listed newest first. Click one to preview it on the right.
  3. Restore this version puts that text back on the page.

While you edit, Vellum saves a snapshot of the previous text at most once every ten minutes, and keeps the last thirty per page. Restoring is itself an edit — so if you restore the wrong one, open history again and restore what you had.

¶

History needs a connection. Snapshots live on the server, not on your device, so the menu item is greyed out while you’re offline. Databases don’t have page history; their rows do.

¶Comments

Every page has a comment panel under the editor, next to Linked mentions.

  • Type in Add a comment… and press Enter or click Send.
  • The tick resolves a comment; resolved ones stay visible but dimmed, and the arrow reopens them.
  • The bin deletes one for good.
  • The header counts the unresolved ones; click it to fold the panel.

Comments also live on the server, so the panel says “Comments unavailable offline” until you reconnect. Commenting on a comment isn’t supported, and the Vault has no comments at all — they couldn’t be encrypted, so they aren’t offered.

Publishing to the web

Any page can become a public web page with one switch. No account needed to read it, and no way in to the rest of your workspace.

¶Publishing

  1. Open the page and click Share in the top bar.
  2. Turn on Publish to web.
  3. A link appears. Copy link puts it on your clipboard; Open shows you what readers see.

The link contains a long random string that nobody can guess — that string is the access control. Anyone you give it to can read the page; anyone who doesn’t have it can’t.

¶Unpublishing

Turn the switch off and the link stops working immediately and permanently — it is destroyed, not hidden. If you publish the same page again you get a different link, so the old one stays dead. Publishing a page that’s already published keeps the link you’ve shared, so a double click can’t break it.

Moving a published page to the trash also takes it offline.

¶What a reader sees

  • The page’s title and content, as plain, fast HTML.
  • Links to your other pages as plain titles, never links — a published page can’t reveal that a private page exists, let alone open it.
  • Nothing else: no sidebar, no comments, no way to edit.
¶

Publishing needs a connection — the switch explains itself while you’re offline. And Vault pages can’t be published at all: the server only ever holds their ciphertext, so there would be nothing to show.

Exporting & importing

Your writing is yours. Every page can leave Vellum as a file, and other people’s files can come in.

¶Exporting a page

Share in the top bar (or …) offers, for a document:

  • Markdown — a .md file named after the page.
  • HTML — a styled, self-contained .html file.
  • PDF — in the Mac app this opens a save dialog; in a browser it hands the same document to the print dialog, where you choose Save as PDF.

For a database there’s one option, Export as CSV: one row per record, one column per property, ready for a spreadsheet.

Files land in your Downloads folder.

¶Copying instead of downloading

… › Copy page contents puts the whole page on the clipboard as Markdown — handy for pasting into an email, a chat or another editor.

¶Importing

  1. Make (or open) the page you want the content to land in.
  2. … › Import Markdown or HTML…
  3. Choose a .md, .markdown, .txt, .html or .htm file.

Headings, lists, quotes, code and links all come across as proper blocks. The import is added to the page you’re on, so import into an empty page unless you mean to append.

¶

Export and import both work on the page currently on screen, so the options are missing when no page is open — and a locked page can’t be imported into until you unlock it.

The Vault

The Vault is the corner of your workspace that is end-to-end encrypted. Pages inside are sealed on your device with a passphrase only you know: the server, the database and the network only ever see ciphertext.

¶Setting it up

  1. Click Create Vault under the Vault heading in the sidebar.
  2. Choose a passphrase — at least 8 characters — and type it twice.
  3. Click Create Vault. It opens unlocked, ready for its first page.
¶

There is no reset and no recovery. The passphrase never leaves your device, so nobody — including Vellum — can unlock the Vault for you. If you forget it, what’s inside is gone. That is the point; write it down somewhere safe.

¶Using it

  • New page inside the Vault works like any other page — it’s encrypted before it leaves the window.
  • The Vault view lists what’s inside, with real titles, while unlocked.
  • Lock now seals it immediately. So does closing or reloading Vellum, and fifteen minutes without activity.
  • Unlocking asks for the passphrase; a wrong one is refused with Wrong passphrase.

While locked, the sidebar shows a Locked chip, and any Vault page you open reads 🔒 Locked page with an unlock form. Nothing of its content is on screen or in memory until you unlock.

¶What the Vault deliberately can’t do

  • Search can’t look inside it — there is no readable text to index. While unlocked you can still find Vault pages by title.
  • Publishing is refused for Vault pages.
  • Comments aren’t offered inside; they couldn’t be encrypted.
  • Databases can’t be created inside the Vault.
  • Mentions can’t cross the boundary, in either direction.
  • Pages can’t be moved in or out. An ordinary page dragged in would leave its old, unencrypted history behind. Create the page inside the Vault instead.
  • Trashed Vault pages appear in the trash as Locked page, with no title.

Settings & your account

⌘,, the gear in the sidebar footer, or ⌘K › Settings.

¶Account

  • Your email — the one the workspace is locked to.
  • Change password — asks for the current one, then the new one twice, with the same live checklist as sign-up. A new password has to meet the policy in full.
  • Sign out everywhere — ends every session on every device, including this one.

¶Security

  • Vault — shows whether it’s locked, with a Lock now button while it’s open. Appears once you’ve made a Vault.
  • Touch ID sign-in — on a Mac with biometrics, removes the saved credentials. To enable it, tick the box while signing in.

¶Appearance

Light or Dark. Vellum follows your system setting until you choose, then remembers.

¶Danger zone

Delete account erases everything: pages, databases, the Vault, uploaded files, history, comments and the account itself, on every device. To go through with it you must enter your password and type DELETE.

¶

There is no undo and no backup unless you made one. Export anything you want to keep first — see Exporting & importing.

Offline & sync

Vellum keeps a full copy of your workspace on every device you use it on. The network makes your devices agree with each other; it is never what makes the app work.

¶What happens when you go offline

  • Everything keeps working: reading, writing, new pages, databases, moving things around.
  • A small chip above the sidebar footer says Offline — changes saved locally, or counts what’s waiting: Offline — 3 changes pending.
  • When you’re back, the queue replays in order and the chip disappears. You don’t press anything.

The chip only shows when there’s something to say — while you’re connected and everything is saved, it stays out of the way.

¶What does need a connection

  • Page history and comments, which live on the server.
  • Publishing and public links.
  • Bookmark previews (the card still appears, just without the fetched title).
  • Account settings.

Each one says so in place rather than failing silently.

¶Editing the same page in two places

If two devices edit the same page while apart, the version saved last wins the whole page when they meet again. Different pages never conflict — they merge cleanly.

¶Installing Vellum

  • Mac app — native menus, its own Dock icon, Touch ID unlock and true PDF export.
  • As an app from your browser — Vellum ships a web app manifest and a service worker, so you can install it from the address bar. It then opens in its own window and boots even with no connection at all.
  • Any browser — just visit the workspace.

All three are the same workspace; edits made in one show up in the others.
